# Reporting, emergencies and corrective action

## Purpose and trigger
Turn verified delivery and progress evidence into controlled reports and timely correction. Trigger this guide for national reporting dates, action-plan communication, complaints, emergencies, incidents, evidence changes and review expiry. The accountable reporting owner controls report preparation; a named authorised human approves the exact submission. The emergency lead owns the applicable rapid-response protocol. Inputs include jurisdiction, national instructions, period inventory, calculations, delivery/viewer findings, unresolved issues and delegation.

## Prepare and approve reports
Use the actual provider timetable and regulator format. Article 7(2)'s Member State reporting cycle is not an automatic provider deadline. Record the national instruction, duty holder, due date, reporting period, calculation method and source currency. Article 7(3) distinguishes encouragement of provider action plans from communication of any such plan to the national authority; national rules may impose further duties.

Reconstruct every material reported figure from the eligible inventory, feature-specific numerator, denominator, units, counting rules, failed/missing assets and delivery evidence. Explain acquisitions, repeats, catalogue growth, rights changes, method changes and differences between produced and usable provision. Preserve quality failures and viewer barriers even when a numerical target is met. Do not publish a single compliance score in place of this evidence.

Approval binds the exact report version or hash, period, figures, scope, limitations, human approver, authority basis, date and validity. Changed figures, wording or period require renewed review. A prepared report is not a submission; submission requires a supported authorised channel and verified receipt. Keep the submitted artifact, transaction reference and receipt together. Check the outcome before retrying an uncertain transmission to avoid duplicates.

## Emergency preparedness and response
Article 7(5) concerns accessible emergency information made public through audiovisual services. Recital 24 recognises exceptional cases where accessible provision may not be possible without preventing emergency information being made public. Verify the operative national procedure and approved rapid-response authority. Do not invent a universal rule holding every public emergency message for normal accessibility sign-off.

Plan accessible message variants, verified essential instructions, language/signing needs, update synchronisation, delivery routes, fallback arrangements, named on-call roles and urgent correction. In controlled exercises, check that people can obtain the actual instruction, place, time and updates. Record version, activation/transmission times, available and missing variants, conflicting information and escalation. Do not send real hazardous public messages as a repository test.

During a real incident, use the existing authorised protocol. Preserve the unresolved accessibility barrier, act on urgent access correction and route specialist legal/editorial judgements to their owners. An agent cannot manufacture emergency authority. Urgency does not make a missing feature a pass. Review incidents afterwards for practical causes and future prevention.

## Complaints and correction
Identify the national Article 7(4) contact point separately from provider support and complaints. Test contact accessibility and whether staff can understand, investigate and communicate resolution. Zero complaints through an inaccessible route is weak evidence. Obtain actual national response duties; do not invent an EU-wide response limit.

Triage by consequence, reach, critical information, repeat failure and affected access approaches. Link the complaint to the exact programme/track/version/route where possible, preserving uncertainty rather than demanding diagnosis. Assign an owner, correction, due date and affected claims. Apply the fix, retest actual delivery and verify the viewer's original barrier where feasible. Record recurrence, escaped barriers, time until access is restored and unresolved harm; closed tickets alone do not measure success.

## Monitor, decide and hand off
Monitoring covers new national rules, source expiry, catalogue edits, acquisition rights, suppliers, packaging, player/device updates, complaints and contradictory research. Reopen affected conclusions and revise or withdraw exact public claims when evidence changes. A claim needs an approved conclusion, supporting evidence and approved wording/publication controls; publication remains a separate authorised operation.

Output reports, approvals, submissions/receipts, emergency records, complaints, issues and monitoring events. Stop the affected report/claim when evidence, authority, national instructions or binding version is missing; continue correction and independent preparation. Route reporting via WF09, emergency exercises/response via WF10, correction/retest via WF11 and reassessment/claim control via WF12. Call the process operational only after real delegated pilot cycles, competent independent review, tested integrations and sustained outcomes have been evidenced.
