# Commissioning, production and acquisition

## Purpose and trigger
Plan accessibility with the programme and verify acquired material before accepting it. Use this guide at commissioning, treatment, supplier selection, acquisition, recut or renewal. The accountable commissioner owns the brief and resources; the provider retains its applicable regulatory responsibilities. Inputs include approved service requirements, intended audiences, languages, editorial purpose, distribution specifications, rights and the exact reference cut where one exists.

## Define acceptance before creation or purchase
For a new production, write feature and audience outcomes into the brief before scripts, graphics and sound are fixed. Include disabled collaborators early in treatment and design. Budget for specialist authoring, translation/signing, independent review, recording, mix, technical validation, audience participation, corrections and redelivery. A short graphic-heavy sequence may need substantial descriptive work.

For an existing programme, inventory actual masters and access assets, check rights and ask the distributor for missing material. A supplier declaration alone does not establish availability, quality or a right to distribute. Contracts should specify programmes/editions, languages, formats/profiles, timing origin, metadata, territories, platforms, archive/reuse rights, delivery dates, review access, correction responsibilities and retest. Record unavailable assets and alternative procurement options; do not promise access without rights or delivery evidence.

## Plan meaning through sound and picture
For reportage, presentation and discussion, use radio/podcast techniques to make essential information available in the main soundtrack. Introduce speakers and places, explain relevant graphics and demonstrations, distinguish observation from assertion, and speak essential calls to action. A chair can identify a meaningful silent reaction naturally. Commentary should orient listeners to current state, action and outcome, distinguish replay from current events and correct uncertainty honestly.

Audit the finished picture against the actual soundtrack. Integrated explanation, separate AD, a hybrid or a supported extended version may be appropriate. Factual description prioritises practical information and evidence. Drama and entertainment also require tone, timing, emotion, suspense and visual humour. Accurate description can be expressive; it should not invent psychology, identities or facts, spoil a reveal or compete with important sound.

An audio-led or no-separate-AD decision requires a version-specific visual audit, independent listening review and editorial approval. It does not override an applicable national AD quota or programme-specific obligation. Check the legal target before choosing the delivery route. A transcript is useful supplementary material, but is not automatically equivalent to synchronised description.

## Coordinate production and suppliers
Provide the access team with reference picture, scripts, running orders, verified names/pronunciations, graphics text and clean audio/stems as appropriate. Caption the final soundtrack, rather than assuming the script matches it. Protect meaningful sound, signer framing and readable graphics. Plan live preparation, operator communication and recovery. Each recut requires an impact review of cues, timing, names, reveals, language, crops and mix.

Name the people occupying these roles in real records. Participants contribute expertise and findings; they do not inherit compliance or risk accountability. A small team can combine roles while documenting the authority exercised and retaining competent challenge.

## Outputs, stops and next workflow
Output programme inventory, feature specifications, editorial decisions, rights records, supplier deliverables and acceptance evidence. Separate supplier acceptance from downstream delivery readiness. Reject or condition acceptance when files mismatch the cut, rights are unclear, required features are absent or quality evidence is inadequate. Correct and retest rather than converting a deadline exception into a pass.

Resolve conventional technical defects before final audience evaluation unless a study deliberately investigates them with appropriate safeguards. Continue independent scripting or inventory work while a specific rights or legal decision is blocked. Route production through WF05, delivery verification through WF06 and viewer evaluation through WF07. Retain supplier correction and monitoring obligations after purchase or release; an accepted file can still fail in distribution.
